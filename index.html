Kendall Notation Explained

M
Arrival Process
Markovian (Poisson) arrivals. Hiring requests arrive randomly and independently at an average rate of λ = 8 per week. No clustering or scheduling — typical of unplanned employee turnover in a BPO.
/
M
Service Process
Markovian (Exponential) service. The time to fill a position follows an exponential distribution with μ = 10 per week. Recruitment times vary — some roles fill quickly, others take longer.
/
1
Servers
Single server. ConnectPro employs only one recruitment specialist to handle all replacement hiring requests across the entire organization.
:
∞
System Capacity
Infinite capacity. There is no limit on how many requests can wait in the queue. No request is ever turned away — they all wait until the specialist can process them.
/
∞
Population
Infinite calling population. The pool of potential departing employees is treated as effectively unlimited. Any employee can generate a replacement request at any time.
/
FCFS
Queue Discipline
First-Come, First-Served. Requests are processed in the order they arrive. No priority is given to specific departments or roles — strictly chronological order.

Strategic Recommendations
👥
Hire a Second Recruitment Specialist
↑ Capacity: c = 1 → 2
Adding a second server (c=2) with the same μ=10 dramatically reduces congestion. The M/M/2 model yields ρ = 0.40 per server, Lq ≈ 0.15, and Wq ≈ 0.02 weeks (~0.10 days). Queue waiting drops by 95%.
🤝
Retention & Workplace Satisfaction Programs
↓ Arrival Rate: λ = 8 → 6
Reducing turnover from 24% to 18% lowers λ to ~6 requests/week. This cuts ρ to 60%, Ls to 1.5, and Wq to 0.15 weeks (0.75 days) — a 62.5% reduction in wait time with the same single specialist.
💻
Applicant Tracking System (ATS)
↑ Service Rate: μ = 10 → 12
An ATS automates resume screening, interview scheduling, and candidate tracking, boosting μ to 12. This cuts ρ to 66.7%, Ls to 2.0, and Wq to 0.17 weeks (0.83 days) — a 58.3% reduction in queue wait.
🎓
Better Benefits & Career Development
↓ Turnover: 24% → 15–18%
Competitive compensation, training, and clear career paths address root causes of attrition. Even a modest turnover reduction to 18% cuts weekly arrivals by 25%, easing the recruitment bottleneck sustainably.
🔍
Key Insights & Interpretation
  • The system is stable but strained. With ρ = 80%, the recruitment specialist is busy four out of every five working hours. While technically stable (ρ < 1), there is very little slack to absorb spikes.
  • Requests wait approximately 2 working days. On average, a hiring request sits in the queue for Wq = 0.4 weeks (2 days) before the specialist even begins processing it. Total time in system is 2.5 days.
  • There is a 51.2% chance of more than 2 requests in the system. More than half the time, the specialist has a backlog of 3+ open requests, creating pressure and potential burnout.
  • The queue is the bottleneck, not the processing. Of the 4 average requests in the system (Ls), 3.2 are waiting (Lq = 80% of Ls). The specialist works efficiently once a request is picked up.
  • Small changes in λ or μ have outsized impact. Due to the nonlinear relationship near ρ = 1, reducing λ by just 2 requests/week (from 8→6) cuts average wait time by over 60%.
  • Adding a second specialist is the most impactful single change, reducing queue wait by approximately 95%, but comes with ongoing headcount cost. Combining an ATS (↑μ) with retention efforts (↓λ) can achieve similar results.
  • Addressing root-cause turnover is the most sustainable strategy. Lowering turnover from 24% to 18% not only reduces recruitment workload but also improves institutional knowledge, team stability, and service quality.
